Complex Numbers and Quadratic Equations: JEE Main Maths formulas and notes

Complex Numbers and Quadratic Equations give 2 to 3 questions in JEE Mains in most years: a simplification or a locus, a power of ω, and a quadratic with a condition on its roots. About 8 hours in 5 days is enough. The chapter is the base for sequences, conics and calculus, so the effort pays again.

Quick revision

Formulas with solved examples

Complex numbers: algebra

Powers of i and the basic operations

  • i² = −1
  • iⁿ depends on n mod 4: i⁰ = 1, i¹ = i, i² = −1, i³ = −i
  • (a + ib) + (c + id) = (a + c) + i(b + d)
  • (a + ib)(c + id) = (ac − bd) + i(ad + bc)
  • a + ib = c + id ⇔ a = c and b = d

How to apply. Divide n by 4 and keep the remainder. For a quotient multiply the top and bottom by the conjugate of the bottom.

Example. i²⁰²³: 2023 leaves remainder 3 on division by 4, so i²⁰²³ = i³ = −i. And (3 + 4i)/(1 − 2i) = −1 + 2i.

Square root of a complex number

  • √(a + ib) = ±( √((|z| + a)/2) + i·sign(b)·√((|z| − a)/2) ) where |z| = √(a² + b²)
  • or put √(a + ib) = x + iy and compare real and imaginary parts

How to apply. Square x + iy, equate the real and imaginary parts, and use x² + y² = |z| as a helper.

Example. √(3 + 4i) = ±(2 + i), because (2 + i)² = 3 + 4i.

Modulus, conjugate and argument

Conjugate and modulus

  • z̄ = x − iy
  • z + z̄ = 2x
  • z − z̄ = 2iy
  • z·z̄ = |z|²
  • |z₁z₂| = |z₁||z₂|
  • |z₁/z₂| = |z₁|/|z₂|
  • |z̄| = |z|
  • |zⁿ| = |z|ⁿ
  • conj of a product = product of conjugates
  • z is real ⇔ z = z̄
  • z is purely imaginary ⇔ z + z̄ = 0 (and z ≠ 0)

How to apply. Use z·z̄ = |z|² to remove a modulus or to divide. Use z = z̄ to test whether a number is real.

Example. |3 − 4i| = 5 and (3 − 4i)(3 + 4i) = 25.

Triangle inequalities

  • |z₁ + z₂| ≤ |z₁| + |z₂|
  • |z₁ − z₂| ≥ ||z₁| − |z₂||
  • |z₁ + z₂|² + |z₁ − z₂|² = 2(|z₁|² + |z₂|²)
  • equality when z₁ and z₂ have the same direction (first one)

How to apply. Use them to find the greatest and least value of |z₁ + z₂| when |z₁| and |z₂| are given.

Example. If |z₁| = 3 and |z₂| = 4 then |z₁ + z₂| lies between 1 and 7.

Argument

  • arg z = θ where tan θ = y/x, with the quadrant of (x, y)
  • principal value −π < θ ≤ π
  • arg(z₁z₂) = arg z₁ + arg z₂ (mod 2π)
  • arg(z₁/z₂) = arg z₁ − arg z₂
  • arg z̄ = − arg z
  • arg(−z) = arg z ± π

How to apply. Draw the point in the plane first to pick the quadrant, then find the angle from tan⁻¹|y/x|.

Example. arg(−1 + i) = 3π/4, since the point is in the second quadrant with a 45° slope.

Polar form, De Moivre and roots of unity

Polar form and De Moivre

  • z = r(cos θ + i sin θ) = r e^{iθ}
  • (cos θ + i sin θ)ⁿ = cos nθ + i sin nθ
  • to multiply, multiply moduli and add arguments
  • to find a power, raise the modulus and multiply the argument

How to apply. Convert to polar form for large powers: find r and θ first.

Example. (1 + i)⁸: modulus √2, argument π/4, so the result has modulus 16 and argument 2π, i.e. 16.

Cube roots of unity

  • ω = (−1 + i√3)/2, ω² = (−1 − i√3)/2
  • ω³ = 1
  • 1 + ω + ω² = 0
  • ω̄ = ω²
  • ωⁿ depends on n mod 3
  • 1 + ω = −ω² and 1 + ω² = −ω

How to apply. Reduce the power of ω with n mod 3. Replace 1 + ω + ω² by 0 whenever it appears.

Example. 1 + ω + ω² = 0, so (1 + ω)⁷ = (−ω²)⁷ = −ω¹⁴ = −ω².

nth roots of unity

  • the roots of zⁿ = 1 are cos(2kπ/n) + i sin(2kπ/n), k = 0, 1, …, n − 1
  • their sum is 0
  • their product is (−1)ⁿ⁺¹
  • they lie on the unit circle and form a regular n-gon

How to apply. Use the sum rule for sums like 1 + ω + ω² + … and the polygon picture for geometry questions.

Example. The 4th roots of unity are 1, i, −1, −i and their sum is 0.

Locus and geometry

Distance and standard loci

  • |z − z₁| = distance between z and z₁
  • |z − z₀| = r is a circle (centre z₀, radius r)
  • |z − z₁| = |z − z₂| is the perpendicular bisector
  • |z − z₁| + |z − z₂| = k (k > |z₁ − z₂|) is an ellipse
  • arg((z − z₁)/(z − z₂)) = constant is an arc of a circle
  • three points are collinear when (z₃ − z₁)/(z₂ − z₁) is real

How to apply. Write z = x + iy and simplify, or read the geometric meaning of the modulus as a distance.

Example. |z − 1| = |z + 1| is the imaginary axis (the perpendicular bisector of 1 and −1).

Quadratic equations: roots and nature

Roots, sum and product

  • ax² + bx + c = 0
  • x = (−b ± √D)/2a with D = b² − 4ac
  • α + β = −b/a
  • αβ = c/a
  • equation with roots α, β: x² − (α + β)x + αβ = 0
  • α² + β² = (α + β)² − 2αβ
  • α³ + β³ = (α + β)³ − 3αβ(α + β)
  • |α − β| = √D/|a|

How to apply. Express the quantity asked in terms of α + β and αβ first, then substitute.

Example. For x² − 6x + 8 = 0: α + β = 6, αβ = 8, so α² + β² = 36 − 16 = 20 and the roots are 2 and 4.

Nature of roots

  • D > 0: two real distinct roots
  • D = 0: equal real roots
  • D < 0: two complex conjugate roots (for real a, b, c)
  • D a perfect square with rational a, b, c: rational roots
  • irrational and complex roots come in conjugate pairs for real or rational coefficients

How to apply. Find D first. For a parameter k, form D as an expression in k and solve the inequality.

Example. x² − 6x + 13 = 0 has D = 36 − 52 = −16 < 0, so the roots are 3 ± 2i.

Location of roots and common roots

Common roots and conditions

  • one common root: eliminate x² between the two equations to get the common root, then substitute
  • both roots common: a₁/a₂ = b₁/b₂ = c₁/c₂
  • roots in a ratio m : n: (m + n)²·ac = mn·b²
  • one root double the other: 2b² = 9ac
  • roots reciprocal: a = c
  • roots equal in size and opposite in sign: b = 0

How to apply. Write the condition in terms of α and β and then convert to a, b, c using the sum and product.

Example. Roots in ratio 1 : 2 give 2b² = 9ac, e.g. x² − 3x + 2 = 0 (roots 1 and 2): 2·9 = 18 = 9·1·2.

Location of roots and sign of a quadratic

  • for f(x) = ax² + bx + c with a > 0: both roots greater than k needs D ≥ 0, −b/2a > k and f(k) > 0
  • k between the roots needs f(k) < 0
  • exactly one root in (p, q) needs f(p)·f(q) < 0
  • f(x) > 0 for all real x needs a > 0 and D < 0

How to apply. Sketch the parabola. Pick out the three things to check: D, the position of the vertex −b/2a, and the sign of f at the given point.

Example. x² − 4x + 5 > 0 for all x because a = 1 > 0 and D = 16 − 20 = −4 < 0.

Most asked question types

How do I solve “Simplify a complex expression or find its modulus and argument” questions in JEE Main?

A fraction or power of complex numbers is given. Write it as x + iy, or find |z| and arg z. 1. For a quotient multiply top and bottom by the conjugate of the bottom. 2. Use z·z̄ = |z|² and the rule |z₁z₂| = |z₁||z₂|. 3. For large powers convert to polar form and use De Moivre. Example: (1 + i)⁸ = 16.

How do I solve “Locus of a point z” questions in JEE Main?

A condition on |z − a| or arg is given. Identify the curve. 1. Read |z − a| as a distance from the point a. 2. Equal distances to two points give a line; a fixed distance gives a circle. 3. Check by writing z = x + iy when in doubt. Example: |z − 1| = |z + 1| is the imaginary axis.

How do I solve “Roots of unity and powers of ω” questions in JEE Main?

An expression in ω, ω² or in nth roots of unity is given. 1. Reduce each power of ω with n mod 3. 2. Replace 1 + ω + ω² by 0 wherever it appears. 3. For nth roots of unity use that their sum is 0. Example: (1 + ω)⁷ = −ω².

How do I solve “Nature, sum and product of roots” questions in JEE Main?

A quadratic with a parameter, or a relation between the roots, is given. 1. Write α + β = −b/a and αβ = c/a. 2. Express the asked quantity through them. 3. Use D = b² − 4ac for the nature of the roots or the range of a parameter. Example: x² − 6x + 8 = 0 gives α² + β² = 20.

How do I solve “Common roots and location of roots” questions in JEE Main?

Two equations share a root, or the roots must lie in a given interval. 1. Eliminate the squared term to get the common root and substitute it. 2. For location use D, the vertex −b/2a and the sign of f at the given point. 3. Sketch the parabola to avoid missing a case. Example: x² − 4x + 5 > 0 for all x (D < 0, a > 0).

Common myths and the truth

  • √(−a)√(−b) = √(ab). Not for negative numbers: √(−1)√(−1) = i·i = −1, not 1.
  • Every quadratic has two real roots. Real roots need D ≥ 0. D < 0 gives a conjugate pair of complex roots.

Mistakes to avoid

  • Forgetting the quadrant when finding arg z.
  • Treating |z|² as z².
  • Using D = b² − 4ac when the coefficients are not real.
  • Missing the case D = 0 in a condition on the roots.
