Neural Control and Coordination (4 simulations)

  • Nerve impulse: the action potential — Stimulate an axon. If the stimulus reaches threshold, Na⁺ rushes in and a full action potential travels…
  • Myelinated vs non-myelinated fibres — Compare how fast impulses travel: continuously along a bare axon or jumping from node to node of Ranvier.
  • Reflex arc: the knee jerk — Tap the patellar tendon. The impulse runs in through a sensory neuron and straight out through a motor…
  • Transmission across a chemical synapse — An impulse arrives at the axon terminal: synaptic vesicles release neurotransmitter, which crosses the cleft…

Excretory Products and their Elimination (3 simulations)

  • ADH and water balance — Drink more or less water and see the hypothalamus adjust ADH, changing urine volume and concentration.
  • Counter-current mechanism — Move a probe down the loop of Henle and read the osmolarity of the medullary interstitium.
  • Nephron: filtration and reabsorption — Trace filtrate from the glomerulus through the tubule. Nearly all of it is reabsorbed.

Body Fluids and Circulation (4 simulations)

  • Blood groups and transfusion — Pick a donor and a recipient. See the antigens on donor red cells and the antibodies in the recipient’s…
  • The cardiac cycle — Watch the heart’s chambers contract and relax in sequence and hear when the valves close (lub, dub).
  • Double circulation — Follow red blood cells through the pulmonary circuit (heart–lungs) and the systemic circuit (heart–body).
  • Electrocardiogram (ECG) — Read an ECG trace: P wave, QRS complex and T wave. Count QRS complexes to find the heart rate.

Animal Kingdom (4 simulations)

  • Body symmetry — Rotate a cutting plane through each animal. Does it give two identical (mirror) halves?
  • Chordates vs non-chordates — Look inside a transparent body and compare the five key differences between chordates and non-chordates…
  • Germ layers and the body cavity — Slice through four body plans and see the germ layers and whether a body cavity (coelom) is present.
  • Phylum explorer — Spin through the major animal phyla and compare their key features (NCERT Table 4.2).

Breathing and Exchange of Gases (4 simulations)

  • Mechanism of breathing (bell-jar model) — Pull the rubber diaphragm down: the volume of the jar increases, pressure falls and the balloons (lungs)…
  • Exchange of gases: partial pressures — Follow blood along a pulmonary or tissue capillary and watch O₂ and CO₂ diffuse down their partial-pressure…
  • Spirometer: lung volumes and capacities — Breathe normally, then take a maximum breath in and out. Read the volumes and capacities from the spirogram.
  • Oxygen–haemoglobin dissociation curve — See how the % saturation of haemoglobin depends on pO₂, and how CO₂, acidity and temperature shift the curve.

Structural Organisation in Animals (2 simulations)

  • Cockroach: organ systems — Explore a cockroach (Periplaneta americana) and highlight each organ system in turn.
  • Epithelial tissues — Build sheets of epithelium from flat, cube-shaped or tall cells, with or without cilia.

Chemical Coordination and Integration (3 simulations)

  • Map of endocrine glands — Locate the endocrine glands in the body and see the main hormones each secretes.
  • Insulin, glucagon and blood glucose — Eat a meal and watch insulin from β-cells bring blood glucose back down; glucagon from α-cells raises it…
  • Thyroid feedback and iodine — Reduce dietary iodine. Less thyroid hormone is made, TSH rises by negative feedback and the thyroid enlarges…

Biomolecules (3 simulations)

  • Temperature and pH affect enzymes — Change temperature and pH and watch an enzyme’s activity rise to an optimum and then fall.
  • Enzyme action and competitive inhibition — Raise the substrate concentration and see the reaction rate level off. Add a competitive inhibitor and…
  • Levels of protein structure — Fold a protein from its amino acid sequence to a helix, a 3D globule and finally a multi-subunit protein.

Locomotion and Movement (3 simulations)

  • The human skeleton: 206 bones — Highlight parts of the axial and appendicular skeleton and count their bones.
  • Sliding filament theory — Contract a sarcomere: thin actin filaments slide over thick myosin filaments, pulling the Z lines together.
  • Synovial joints — Move five kinds of synovial joint and see which directions each allows.
