Class 11 Physics simulations

Thermodynamics (9 simulations)

  • Adiabatic compression and expansion — Move an insulated piston and trade mechanical work with the gas’s internal energy.
  • Carnot heat engine — Follow two isotherms and two adiabats around a reversible cycle.
  • First law of thermodynamics — Add heat and let the gas do work; track its internal energy.
  • Heating gas at constant pressure — Weights hold the piston pressure fixed while you heat the gas. Track Q, W and ΔU.
  • Entropy in isothermal expansion — Expand an ideal gas reversibly at constant temperature and connect heat to entropy.
  • Petrol engine (Otto cycle) — Compress the fuel–air mixture more and the ideal efficiency rises — watch the piston and crank turn.
  • Work from a rectangular P–V cycle — Take a monatomic gas round a rectangular cycle. The enclosed area is the net work per cycle.
  • Refrigerator and heat pump — Pump heat from a cold box to a warm room. The ideal coefficient of performance depends only on the…
  • Gas under a piston — Compress or heat an ideal gas and watch its pressure respond.

Mechanical Properties of Fluids (10 simulations)

  • Lift on an aerofoil — Air flows faster over the curved top of a wing. Bernoulli’s principle gives the pressure difference.
  • Float or sink? — Change the densities and watch a block find its level in a fluid.
  • Capillary rise and depression — Dip a narrow tube in a liquid and see surface tension lift or depress the meniscus.
  • Flow through a narrowing pipe — Change pipe areas and compare the two fluid speeds.
  • Pressure inside drops and bubbles — Surface tension squeezes a drop. A soap bubble has two surfaces, so its excess pressure doubles.
  • Hydraulic lift (Pascal) — Press a small piston and lift a big load on the wide piston.
  • Pressure at depth in a tank — Lower a pressure probe into a tank and change the liquid. Find the force on one side wall too.
  • Viscous drag and terminal speed — Release a small sphere in thick liquid and watch drag grow until the forces balance.
  • Water jet from a tank — Change the water head and follow the jet from a small hole.
  • Venturimeter — Water speeds up through a narrow throat and its pressure drops — read the drop on the vertical tubes.

Motion in a Plane (10 simulations)

  • Projectile with air resistance — Add linear air drag and compare the path with the ideal vacuum parabola.
  • Uniform circular motion — Watch speed stay constant while velocity changes direction.
  • Conical pendulum — Whirl a bob in a horizontal circle and see how the cone angle sets the speed and period.
  • Horizontal projectile — Launch a ball horizontally from a cliff and watch its parabolic drop.
  • Projectile motion — Launch an object and see how horizontal and vertical motion combine to form its path.
  • Projectile landing on a slope — Launch above a hillside and locate the point where the projectile meets the rising ground.
  • Six particles chasing on a hexagon (HC Verma) — Six particles at the corners of a regular hexagon of side a each move at constant speed v toward the…
  • Three particles chasing each other (HC Verma) — Three particles at the corners of an equilateral triangle each move at speed v straight toward the next…
  • Boat crossing a river — Steer upstream and combine boat velocity with river flow.
  • Vector addition: the parallelogram — Resolve two vectors into components and build their resultant, head to tail.

Laws of Motion (10 simulations)

  • Angle of repose — Tilt a rough plane until the block begins to slide. The critical angle reveals μs.
  • Atwood machine — Connect two hanging masses over a pulley and predict which side descends.
  • Banked road turn — Bank a circular road so a car can turn without friction.
  • Two connected blocks — Pull a pair of blocks joined by a string and find the shared acceleration.
  • Apparent weight in an elevator — Accelerate an elevator and see how the floor’s normal reaction changes a weighing-scale reading.
  • Forces and friction — Push a block and see when the applied force overcomes friction.
  • Recoil of a gun — Fire a bullet from a gun on a frictionless cart. Momentum conservation fixes the recoil speed.
  • Impulse: catching an egg — Stop a falling object over a short or long time and compare the average force needed.
  • Block on an incline — Resolve gravity along a slope and include friction.
  • A load held by two ropes — Change the symmetry of two supporting ropes and calculate their tensions.

System of Particles and Rotational Motion (9 simulations)

  • Conservation of angular momentum — Pull two rotating masses inward and watch the angular speed increase.
  • Centre of mass of three masses — Change three masses on a light triangular plate. The plate balances only on its centre of mass.
  • Moment of inertia of shapes — Compare how mass distribution changes rotational inertia.
  • Parallel-axis theorem — Move a rotation axis away from the centre of a uniform disc and feel its growing inertia.
  • A rod as a physical pendulum — Swing a uniform rod about one end and connect rotational inertia with its oscillation period.
  • Rolling without slipping — Compare the motion of the centre and a point on a wheel.
  • Rolling race: ring, disc, spheres — Release four shapes of equal radius down the same incline. The moment of inertia decides the winner.
  • Torque and rotation — Apply a tangential force at different distances from a pivot and spin the disc.
  • Topple or slide? — Push a tall block with a growing force. It either slides first or tips over its front edge first.

Motion in a Straight Line (8 simulations)

  • Average vs instantaneous velocity — Shrink the time interval and watch the secant slope approach the tangent slope on the x–t graph.
  • Reaction and braking distance — Follow a car through reaction time, braking, and a complete stop.
  • The catch-up problem — Release a faster cart after a delay and find where it catches a moving target.
  • Motion in one dimension — Follow a cart and watch its position graph curve as its velocity changes.
  • Rain and the tilted umbrella — Walk through vertical rain and find the umbrella tilt that keeps you dry using relative velocity.
  • Relative velocity — Compare two carts moving along parallel tracks.
  • Vertical throw and the turning point — Throw a ball upward and follow height, velocity, and acceleration through its highest point.
  • Area under a v–t graph — Read displacement as the area beneath a velocity–time graph.

Work, Energy and Power (9 simulations)

  • Ballistic pendulum — Embed a projectile in a suspended block and infer the height it can swing.
  • Elastic collision — Follow two masses before and after a head-on elastic collision.
  • Conservation of energy — Watch gravitational potential energy turn into kinetic energy on a frictionless track.
  • Power of a lifting motor — Raise a load at constant speed and compare work with the rate of doing work.
  • Glancing collision of two balls — Strike a stationary ball off-centre. Vary the impact offset and the coefficient of restitution.
  • Spring launcher and energy transfer — Release a compressed spring and watch elastic potential energy turn into the cart’s kinetic energy.
  • Work done by a variable force — A force that grows with displacement pushes a block. The work is the area under the F–x graph.
  • Motion in a vertical circle — Whirl a stone on a string in a vertical circle. Below √(5gR) at the bottom, the string goes slack.
  • Work done by a force at an angle — Change the direction of a force and see how much work it does.

Waves (11 simulations)

  • Beats: two notes, one pulsing sound — Superpose two nearby frequencies and see the amplitude rise and fall in beats.
  • Doppler effect — Move a sound source and watch its wavefronts crowd together ahead.
  • Open and closed organ pipes — Choose a pipe type and harmonic. See the standing wave of air displacement inside the pipe.
  • Pulse reflection at a boundary — Send a pulse between two strings and compare its reflected amplitude and sign.
  • Resonance tube: speed of sound — Hold a vibrating tuning fork over a tube whose air column is set by a water level. Find the first and second…
  • Ripple tank: two-source interference — Two dippers make circular waves on water. Watch lines of calm water appear where the waves cancel.
  • Sonometer: frequency and length — Press a vibrating tuning fork on the sonometer box and move the bridge until the paper rider on the wire…
  • Standing waves on a string — Form nodes and antinodes between two fixed ends.
  • Wave speed on a stretched rope — Flick a rope and time the pulse. The speed depends on tension and mass per unit length.
  • Superposition of two waves — Add two travelling waves and watch the resultant form.
  • Travelling wave — Change amplitude, wavelength and frequency to see a wave move through a medium.

Thermal Properties of Matter (9 simulations)

  • Bimetallic strip — Bond two metals and heat them. The one that expands more ends up on the outside of the curve.
  • Mixing hot and cold water — Watch two water samples approach their equilibrium temperature.
  • Thermal expansion — Heat a metal rod and measure the small increase in its length.
  • Steady heat conduction — Build a temperature gradient across a wall and follow heat from the hot face to the cold face.
  • Heating curve: ice to steam — Heat ice at constant power and track temperature through melting and boiling.
  • Newton’s law of cooling — Watch a hot body approach room temperature.
  • Heating with specific heat — Add heat to a substance and compute its temperature rise.
  • Net thermal radiation — Compare radiation emitted by a warm surface with radiation absorbed from its surroundings.
  • Anomalous expansion of water — Water is densest at 4 °C. Cool a pond and see why lakes freeze from the top down.

Kinetic Theory (7 simulations)

  • Brownian motion — A pollen-sized particle is jostled by invisible molecules. Its mean-square displacement grows linearly with…
  • Equipartition of energy — Compare internal energy for different active degrees of freedom.
  • Pressure vs temperature — Heat a sealed rigid container and watch the pressure climb.
  • Molecules in motion — Heat a gas and watch its particles move faster inside a container.
  • Maxwell–Boltzmann speed distribution — Compare a gas at two temperatures and distinguish most probable, mean, and RMS speeds.
  • Molecular mean free path — Follow a molecule through successive collisions in a dilute gas.
  • Pressure from molecular collisions — Molecules strike the walls of a box. Each bounce delivers momentum 2mv_x; together they make pressure.

Mechanical Properties of Solids (9 simulations)

  • Compression and bulk modulus — Apply uniform pressure and compare a material’s change in volume.
  • Elastic energy stored in a wire — Load a wire and calculate the strain energy stored per unit volume.
  • Stretching a spring — Pull the block and see how spring stiffness sets its extension.
  • Poisson’s ratio: stretch and thin — Stretch a wire and watch it get thinner. The lateral strain is a fixed fraction of the longitudinal strain.
  • Shear deformation — Push sideways on a bonded block and connect shear stress to angular deformation.
  • Springs in series and parallel — Join two springs and compare how much the same load stretches the combination.
  • Stress–strain curve — Load a wire and trace it through its elastic and plastic regions.
  • Thermal stress in a clamped rod — Heat a rod held between rigid walls. It cannot expand, so a large compressive stress builds up.
  • Young’s modulus — Stretch a rod under tensile load.

Oscillations (9 simulations)

  • Coupled pendulums — Join two pendulums with a weak spring. Energy slowly swaps between them in beats.
  • Damped harmonic motion — Add velocity-dependent resistance to a spring–mass system.
  • Driven oscillations and resonance — Drive a damped oscillator near its natural frequency and watch its steady-state response build in amplitude.
  • Simple pendulum — Set the length and release angle to watch a pendulum swing.
  • Energy exchange in SHM — Watch kinetic and potential energy trade places as a mass oscillates.
  • Displacement, velocity, acceleration — Follow the three SHM curves and see how they line up in phase.
  • Mass–spring oscillation — Watch a mass oscillate around a spring’s equilibrium position.
  • Oscillating liquid in a U-tube — Displace the liquid in a U-tube and release it. The column oscillates in simple harmonic motion.
  • Mass on a vertical spring — Hang a mass on a spring: it settles at a new equilibrium and oscillates about it.

Units and Measurements (7 simulations)

  • Dimensional analysis of a pendulum — Test which quantities can set a pendulum’s period by matching dimensions on both sides.
  • Propagating errors: density of a cube — Measure a cube’s side and mass with instrument uncertainty and see how the errors combine in ρ = m/a³.
  • Parallax: view from the right angle — Move the viewing direction and see why a raised pointer must be read straight on.
  • Micrometer screw gauge — Hold a real micrometer screw gauge: turn the thimble on the stage (drag up/down or sideways) until the…
  • Spherometer — Lower the central screw onto a curved lens and calculate its radius of curvature from the sagitta.
  • Measurement uncertainty — Combine length uncertainties when calculating an area.
  • Vernier caliper — Hold a real vernier caliper: drag the sliding jaw on the stage until it touches the object, then read the…

Gravitation (7 simulations)

  • Falling through a tunnel in the Earth — Drop a ball into a straight frictionless tunnel through a uniform planet. It oscillates in SHM.
  • Escape from a planet — Explore escape speed for planets with different mass and radius.
  • Geostationary orbit — Pick an orbital period and find the radius where a satellite keeps pace with the turning Earth.
  • Satellite orbit — Move a satellite to another altitude and compare its speed and orbital period.
  • Gravity above and below Earth — Move a probe from inside Earth into space and compare the two different gravity laws.
  • Kepler’s elliptical orbit — Follow a planet around an elliptical orbit with the star at one focus.
  • Energy of a circular orbit — Raise a satellite and split its energy into kinetic and potential parts.
